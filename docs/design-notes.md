## Reference documents (real examples, in `data/source/reference/`)

The user's real paperwork (3 Nov 2026). Use these as the reference whenever working on reading orders, packing lists or in-bounds. Real business data: never commit, publish or put them in the demo.

- **Sales order / packing slip** (`sales-orders/`, PDF): sent by the brand (Deccan Foods) for a store order. Header: date, sales order #, bill to, ship to, PO #, ship method. One line per product: line #, the full item text in bold (`Rice:Deccan:10LBS*4:1053`, `Rice:Deccan:20lbs*2:1006` (size case varies), `NKB:Regular:1576`; the last part is the item code, and the text doesn't always match the product list's item text, e.g. `NKB:Regular:1576` vs `NKBR:400gms*20:1576:Osmania`, so match by code), the description below it, weight and quantity in cases. Can run over several pages; total weight at the end.
- **Packing list of incoming containers** (`packing-lists/`, .xlsx, "NY Transit Containers"): one row per product (#, product code, category, size, brand, product name) and one column per container, with header rows for the invoice/ref number, an order note, the shipping line, the arrival (ETA) date and the container number; each cell is cases of that product in that container; a total row at the bottom. The product codes match A3K's product list (Deccan, Deccan Fresh, Anaganaga, Cafe Nilofer, Bambino); a few rows have no code (e.g. Mayuri clothes). This is what's coming in, before it arrives.
- **A3K inbound report** (`inbound-reports/`, .xlsx, one per container per target customer; the same container can be split across several customers): header with SO/PO #, ref #, container #, incoming date, dry/frozen, terms of delivery, target customer, delivery (trucker), floor load, 20/40 ft, FDA hold, demurrage. Lines: #, item as `Brand:Product` with no item code (the brands in the examples aren't in A3K's product list yet), lot/batch, best-before, pack size, then three groups: scheduled (qty, bags/pallet, total pallets), actually received (qty, bags/pallet, total pallets, damages) and new palletization (bags/pallet, TI × HI, total pallets, total cases). Footer: extra pallets used, BOLs, container inspection (roof, floor, debris, odor, pest activity), received by, final verification by, comments, new pallets used, crossdock or storage, tolls, and full/partial pallets made.

## WMS app design

- Multi-tenant groundwork (`docs/multi-tenant-design.md`, Phase 0): nothing about one warehouse is built into the app. At startup `main.tsx` loads the tenant (`app/src/tenant/index.ts`: `config.json`, `products.json`, `logos.json` from `public/tenants/<id>/`; `VITE_TENANT`, default `a3k`) before any screen; `data/catalog.ts` exposes it to the screens. Location codes are built and read only through `tenant/layout.ts` (`slotCode`, `spotCode`, `normalizeLocation`, …) from `layout.codeFormat`; never write a code with a template string. Client and group IDs are strings from the config. Sample data is the warehouse's optional `seed.json` (format: `Seed` in `tenant/types.ts`; relative days/hours so it always looks current; products by code), turned into a new vault's state by `store/sample.ts` and `chat/sampleChat.ts`; without one a warehouse starts empty (team channel only) and Settings has no "Reset sample data". Each warehouse's vault, sign-in and session are saved under its own names on the device (`storageKey`; A3K keeps the old unscoped names, `storage.unscopedKeys`). The warehouse's own name counts as a common password. `tests/tenant-seed.test.js` checks every seed points at real products, slots and people, and that the demo has nothing of A3K's.
- Frosted-glass look over moving colour, light and dark themes. Frost levels (light, frosted, heavy) change the glass tint, blur and saturation together. The Moving background setting overrides the system reduce-motion preference (it only sets the default). On phones and tablets (coarse pointer) it starts off, and the colour shift (hue animation) runs only on computers: moving colour makes every pane of glass re-blur each frame, which made phones slow. Each screen in `App.tsx` is lazy-loaded (`screens`), and all of them load in the background 1.5 s after sign-in. Phones (≤ 760 px) show less at once: smaller titles, the admin's role switcher only in Settings, a section's tabs under its title, number tiles two per row, home update cards as title + count (tap to open), and Stock by brand with brand, cases, pallets and expired only. A crash shows an error card with Reload instead of a blank screen (`ErrorBoundary`); after a deploy, a missing script or screen file makes the page reload itself at a fresh address (`public/theme.js`, `utils/freshLoad.ts`). Opening the saved session gives up after 4 s if IndexedDB doesn't answer (some iPhones), and "Opening…" turns into a Reload card after 8 s; the demo also offers "Start over on this device". Routes accept the on-screen spelling (`#/in-bounds`, `#/Out-bounds`). All colours are CSS tokens in `app/src/styles/global.css`; style through `.glass` and the tokens, never literal colours in components.
- Fonts ship with the app (Inter for everything, with optical sizing for big headings; Geist Mono for the ticker); no outside requests.
- Home (Oct 2026 rework): no activity ticker; a new person's home has no widgets, they pick them in the first-time tour or with "Widgets" (Messages and Stock by brand are widgets too; widgets have no hide button, they're chosen with "Widgets"); Messages can be tucked away to the side and brought back with a tab on the right edge (`messagesTucked`); owners' announcements show on top of home and Work. No underlined text anywhere (links show by colour). Older text: "Welcome Back <name>!", In-bounds / Inventory / Out-bounds / Messages buttons on the left (a bottom bar on phones), update cards (all from live data: orders going out, due soon and to approve, deliveries arriving and to confirm, expiry, returns, count differences to approve), the save-status pill, and the activity ticker bottom right (latest movements, new and shipped orders, deliveries on the dock, and a few facts; it scrolls only when there's more than fits). On screens 1300 px and wider, Messages and the ticker share a right-hand rail; narrower, Messages is a full-width card at its place in the card order; on phones it's a compact card (unread count, latest mentions). Home settings (theme, glass strength, moving background, which cards show and in what order, Messages settings) are saved per device until sign-in exists.
- Respect reduced motion: animations stop when the system asks.
- Every product box uses `ProductInput` (built on `Autocomplete`): the rest of the best match shows in grey, Tab or → accepts, ↑ ↓ and Enter pick from the list. The shipment's client ranks first; DONOTUSE/CROSSDOCK codes sink.
- In-bounds (`#/inbounds/…`): Dashboard, Incoming, Pending and Returns tabs at the bottom. Incoming → "Mark as arrived" → Pending → owner confirms → put-away plan → stored. "Mark as arrived" keeps the cases per product as `announced` (never edited again; `server/src/shared/receiving.ts`); Pending edits what was received, shows announced against received, and a difference needs a reason to confirm (kept on the delivery and in Logs; the server refuses a confirm without one). "Split into pallets" (Incoming and Pending) makes full pallets of the product's size plus one partial (TI × HI fills the size), and the client portal fills full pallets first when the pallet count fits (`server/src/shared/pallets.ts`). Gaps against A3K's real paperwork: `docs/reference-gaps.md`. Returns stay pending until an owner approves (back into stock creates a pending in-bound; write-off or back-to-brand don't touch inventory) or rejects.
- Put-away (`app/src/store/putaway.ts`): one empty slot per pallet. Preference: next to the same product (same rack, nearest column, then the other side), then the same brand, then the same client, then the first empty slot; lower levels first. No empty slot: the floor of the rack side holding most of that product. A person may move a pallet to another empty slot on the rack view; a reason is required. Lines with the same pallet number are one mixed pallet; damaged cases stay out of put-away.
- Sign-in and accounts (`app/src/auth/`, `#/account`). Until the server exists, everything is on this device:
    - Nothing shows until someone signs in. On the demo site the only account at first is `admin` / `password` (an owner, told to change that password); everywhere else a new device asks the first person to make the owner account ("Set up this device", `SetUpDevice` in `pages/login/LoginScreen.tsx`), which gets the admin powers. There is no built-in password outside the demo. Others use "Ask for an account" on the sign-in screen; an owner approves (choosing role and which clients they may see), rejects, resets a password (temporary, shown once) or turns an account off on the Account page. Approving, resetting and turning on ask for the owner's password again.
    - All app data (stock, orders, messages, accounts, the security log) is one AES-256-GCM "vault" in localStorage (`wms.vault.v1`); nothing is saved unencrypted (the old plain `wms.store.v1` is moved in and deleted). Passwords are never stored: PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 iterations, random salt) turns a password into the key that unlocks that account's copy of the vault key (`wms.auth.v1` holds only salts, ciphertext and hashed usernames). Account requests are encrypted to the vault's public key (ECDH P-256), so only a signed-in owner can read them; approval gives the new account the vault key encrypted to its own public key.
    - 5 wrong tries lock that username for 30 s, doubling to 15 min; unknown usernames take as long as known ones. Sessions keep a non-extractable key in IndexedDB; sign-out after 30 min idle (7 days with "Keep me signed in") and at most 12 h (30 days). New passwords: 12+ characters, not common, not containing the username. The built app sends a Content-Security-Policy (no outside requests, no inline scripts; `vite.config.ts`).
    - Limits, until the server: someone who controls the device or the app's code can get around a browser-only sign-in; turning an account off removes its key on this device but doesn't change the vault key; no two-factor yet; lockout counters live on the device.
    - Account requests no longer ask for a job: the admin picks Owner, Coordinator or Worker (with a description of each) and which clients the person may see.
    - Only the admin account (the first one, `admin`; no other account can get this) can switch, for testing and debugging, to any role as itself (Owner, Coordinator, Worker) or to any person, from the top bar or Settings. Changes made while switched are logged as "Admin (testing as worker)" / "Admin (viewing as Omar)". Other owners never get the switcher, and a switch left saved on the device is ignored for them.
    - The admin can "View the app as" a sample person (the seed's people; A3K: owners Karthik and Maya, coordinators Leo and Nina, workers Omar and Tess, Tess seeing only Deccan and Praj; the demo has Sam instead of Karthik) to check what others see. "Reset sample data" starts the sample data over (accounts stay).
- Inventory (`#/inventory/…`, `app/src/pages/inventory/`, helpers in `app/src/inventory/`): three tabs at the bottom.
    - Products: search by code or name (sorted A–Z, or by code when the text starts like a code), filter (stock, expiry, partial, client, brand) and sort. Picking a product or "All <brand>" highlights every pallet on a whole-warehouse map (one square per slot, tinted by expiry, expired in black) and the rack view, or lists them in a table (oldest arrival first). "Export stock report" makes an .xlsx per chosen brand(s): cases per product, a by-brand sheet, and every pallet location; "One file per brand" keeps brands apart. "Edit product" (the "Edit products" switch: owners, coordinators if an owner turns it on; only brands they may see) changes a product's code, name and full code description (the item text; a new code replaces the old one inside it): edit → review before/after → reason → save, logged under Logs → Products. The id never changes, so stock, orders and messages stay with the product. Saved in the vault (`productEdits`, applied by `applyProductEdits` in `data/catalog.ts`) or on the server (`products.update`; every edit kept in `product_changes`; an edit made from an old copy is refused). Codes may repeat within a client (BOGO packs, `XXXX`).
    - Inventory: every slot and floor spot with occupancy, partial, mixed, expiring and expired counts; a searchable, sortable table (exportable, printable) or rack by rack with fill levels. Any location opens a detail drawer: each product's code, brand, lot, best-before, arrival, delivery, full-pallet size, history and messages; arrow keys step to the next slot. "Move pallet" moves everything to an empty slot or floor spot, with a required reason, logged as a `move`.
    - Count: an owner starts a count (whole warehouse or chosen racks, optionally blind). Everyone counts with the keyboard, with the same keys as the Warehouse Count page (arrows, Shift+arrows, Page Up/Down, type to search, Enter, Tab for a mixed pallet, `=`, `-`, Alt+A, Cmd/Ctrl+C/X/V incl. pasting rows from Excel, Del, Cmd/Ctrl+Z, `?`). An owner reviews the differences and applies corrections (logged as `count-correction` with the reason) or keeps the system stock; uncounted slots never change.
    - .xlsx files are written by `app/src/utils/xlsx.ts` (no library, no outside request): grey bold header like the stock workbooks, frozen header, filters.
    - SAMPLE: the 27 Sep sheets have no lots or best-before dates, so sheet stock gets made-up ones (`withSampleDates` in `app/src/store/sample.ts`) to test expiry screens.
- Out-bounds (`#/outbounds/…`, `app/src/pages/outbounds/`, logic in `app/src/outbound/`): Dashboard, Orders, Inbox and Labels tabs.
    - The reader (`outbound/readText.ts`, pure, tested in `tests/order-reader.test.js`; `reader.ts` wraps it) reads brands' sales orders: the code inside `a:b:…:code` item text, two-line rows, `55.00` quantities, the header repeated on each page, SALES ORDER # (`salesOrder` on the order), DATE as the order date (not the due date), SHIP TO as the store; a labelled due date only.
    - Orders come in three ways, always as a draft that a coordinator or owner approves: typed in (New order), read from a PDF (text read with pdf.js; codes, cases, store, due date and PO number filled in; lines matched by name are flagged; scanned PDFs have no text and are typed in), or from an email to the order inbox (`orders@a3kimports.com` once the server exists; until then sample emails and "Paste an email"). The client portal creates drafts the same way (source "portal"). Status: draft → approved → picking → picked → shipped (or cancelled; picked cases go back on their pallets).
    - Approving plans the picks (`app/src/outbound/plan.ts`): FIFO by arrival date; expired stock is never planned; cases planned for other open orders are reserved; by default open (partial) pallets are used up before a full pallet is opened, so one pallet is taken from until it's empty ("Strictly oldest stock first" is the per-order alternative). Not enough stock = "short".
    - The order page shows the pick list in walking order (by location) with how many cases to take, what's left on the pallet, lot, best-before and arrival; the map highlights every pallet to pick (numbered in walking order; click a product to show only its pallets). Confirming a pick takes the cases off stock (logged as `pick`, undo logged as `pick-undo`). Taking fewer than planned needs a reason; that pallet is skipped for the order, flagged for a recount, and the rest is planned from the next oldest pallet. Pick list as .xlsx or printed.
    - Labels tab: case labels like the Order Label Maker (same sheet, layout, logos and footers; `app/src/outbound/labelsPdf.ts` with jsPDF), one per case, filled from an order (each pallet's best-before on its cases), from a PDF order slip or packing list ("Read a PDF order…", same reader as orders; best-before from the oldest pallet in stock; unread lines listed, other clients' lines left out) or products added by hand (the add box empties after Enter/Tab; Enter in the empty box adds the last product again); the footer box is labelled "Footer" (one per logo when lines use several logos); preview, PDF download, print, alignment test, start position for part-used sheets. Logos come from `data/source/logos` via the tenant's `logos.json`; the sheet, colours and footers from `labels` in its config.
    - Libraries added for this (bundled, loaded only when used, no outside requests): `pdfjs-dist` 4.10.38 (reading PDFs, label preview) and `jspdf` 4.2.1 (making label PDFs).
- Language: English or Español (Settings → Appearance, and on the sign-in screen; saved per device in `wms.lang`). Screens are written in English; in Spanish `app/src/i18n/index.ts` watches the page and swaps interface text, placeholders, titles and aria-labels using `app/src/i18n/es.ts` (exact phrases in `ES`, texts with numbers or names in `ES_PATTERNS`, `{n?plural:singular}` for Spanish endings). Words: pallet = tarima, slot = espacio, case = caja, pick = surtir, put away = acomodar, informal "tú". Never translated: anything under `translate="no"` (what people type in Messages, task reasons, email and PDF text), product, brand, store and people's names, codes, sample data, the Logs action text (a permanent record) and every exported or printed file (.xlsx, labels). Text for `window.confirm`/`prompt` goes through `t()`. `<option>`s need an explicit `value` or they stay English. When adding interface text, add its Spanish to `es.ts`; in dev, `window.__wmsMissing` lists text seen on screen with no translation.
- Permissions: every "may this person see or do this" check goes through `app/src/auth/permissions.ts` (`can`, `canSeeClient`, `visibleLines`), following the owner-defined roles above ("supervisor" was renamed "coordinator"; saved data is migrated). Owners and coordinators see In-bounds, Inventory, Out-bounds, Tasks, Messages and Logs; workers see Work, Inventory and Messages. Only owners decide returns, start and approve counts, adjust stock and manage accounts.
- Pallet tasks (`app/src/store/tasks.ts`, `#/tasks`, `#/work`): confirming a delivery creates one put-away task per pallet (receiving floor → the slot the planner chose); stock is added only when a worker confirms "it's in the slot". Moves: workers ask the WMS for a slot (nearest free on the same rack side) and get their own task; coordinators may choose the slot and give it to a worker or record a move they did. Slots promised to open tasks count as taken for every planner. "Slot is blocked" makes the WMS give the next free slot. Coordinators give tasks and orders to pack to workers on the Tasks page (and the order page); workers pack only orders given to them.
- Work screen (`#/work`, workers' home): clock, my tasks, tasks anyone can take, orders to pack, message alerts, "Move a pallet". Forklift mode (`#/work/forklift`): full screen, very large type, one task at a time with big buttons, next tasks, the latest message; keeps the screen awake.
- Owners see "Manage warehouse" in the menu instead of Logs (and the old Team page). Slot code formats are set by the Lattice team only (ops console → warehouse → Slot codes, only while there's no stock; `setCodeFormat` in `server/src/core/setup.ts`); owners can't change them. Email routing rules (`outbound/routing.ts`) sort inbox mail by client and order/delivery notice; routed senders are trusted on the server. Tasks: `#/tasks/workers` (status and current task per person), `#/tasks/teams` (work teams; a task given to a team is taken by any member), and a "Which pallet" note on move tasks. Messages: anyone starts DMs and group chats from the people directory (`#/messages/new`); a group's creator renames it, changes members or deletes it. New synced kinds: `setting`, `notice`, `team`.
- Logs (`#/logs`, `app/src/store/audit.ts`): every change is recorded automatically by the store (action in words, who, date and time; never edited, kept through "Reset sample data"). Owners see everything plus sign-ins and account changes; coordinators see the pallet log (put-away, moves, picks). Messages are not logged. Changes made while "viewing as" someone are logged as "<owner> (viewing as <person>)". Owners' direct stock changes ("Adjust stock here", in a slot's details) need: edit → review before/after → reason → confirm.
- Home for owners and coordinators adds "Stock by brand · live" (cases, pallets, products, expiring, expired per brand).
- Messages (`#/messages`, `app/src/chat/`, `app/src/components/chat/`): channels (team, one per shift), direct messages, and a thread per order and per delivery (members limited to people who may see that client). Chat never changes stock.
    - Messages are text plus structured references (`MessageRef`: slot, product, person, group, order, delivery, container) saved by id with a small snapshot, so chips show live state, "when sent: …" if it changed, and renames don't break old messages. Typed location codes (padded or old unpadded, Door 6, junctions, floor spots, areas) become slot references and are saved padded; invalid codes stay plain text.
    - Chips: a slot opens the map (`MapOverlay`, built on `RackView`) with the slot pulsing and a card of what's there; several slots get numbered markers in the order written; a product shows every location tinted by expiry (expired in black), partial pallets marked, and a FIFO list (oldest arrival first). Other clients' stock always shows as "hidden", in chips, maps, previews and search.
    - Composer: `@` people and groups (@owners, @coordinators, @receiving, @shift-am, @shift-pm), `#` products/orders/deliveries/containers, 📍 slot picker (dropdowns or tap the map), photos (camera on phones), urgent flag. Urgent mentions still unread after N minutes call the `Notifier` interface (`chat/notify.ts`, a console stub until texts/emails exist).
    - A message counts as read only once at least 60% of it has been on screen with the app in front. Presence: Online, Away (idle 5 min or app in background), Offline · syncing later, Offline; rough times only, latest only; manual statuses (On forklift, On break, Receiving at Door N, Off today). Exact times are limited by a setting (owners + coordinators by default).
    - Everything the screens ask of a server goes through the `ChatBackend` interface (`chat/backend.tsx`). The local version simulates other people (presence heartbeat, reads), and queues messages while offline; Settings → Developer → "Simulate offline" tests it. A real backend (AWS + WebSockets) replaces it without changing the screens. No end-to-end encryption.

## Server on AWS (Phase 1, `docs/multi-tenant-design.md`)

- Database size and standby (owner's decision, 23 Oct 2026): prod starts on a small database (`t4g.large`) with no standby while there are up to ~5 warehouses; scaling up is the GitHub variables `WMS_DB_SIZE` / `WMS_DB_STANDBY` and a deploy (`infra/lib/settings.ts`, `docs/aws-setup.md`).
- Everything runs on AWS, never on a computer: two environments, `staging` and `prod`, from the same code. Deploys go through `.github/workflows/deploy-aws.yml` (by hand) in the order data stack → image → migration loop → API stack, so new code never meets an old database.
- **RDS Proxy from day one** (owner's decision, 15 Oct 2026): the API connects only to the proxy (TLS, IAM sign-in; no database passwords in the app); the proxy caps the database at 70% of `max_connections` and makes requests wait instead of crashing it. In the app, at most 3 connections per tenant pool and 30 per task; least recently used idle pools close; when all are busy the API answers 503 "try again". Never use session `SET`, temp tables, `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY`, advisory locks or named prepared statements through the proxy (they pin connections); per-tenant limits are role settings (`ROLE_LIMITS` in `server/src/migrate/main.ts`: 20 connections, 15 s statements).
- One PostgreSQL database per tenant (`t_<id>`, login `t_<id>`, `CONNECT` revoked from everyone else, read/write on tables only); the platform database (`platform`, read by the API as `wms_platform`) holds tenants, addresses, flags and the migration record.
- **Migrations**: numbered SQL files, never edited after they run (checksums), only adding things. The loop runs platform first, then every tenant (4 at a time), takes a lock per database, one transaction per file, and stops starting tenants after the first failure. Migrations and tenant creation connect straight to the instance as the master login; nothing else does.

- **Sign-in on the server** (`server/src/core/auth.ts`): username + password, then a 6-digit code from an authenticator app (TOTP) for everyone; 5 wrong tries lock for 30 s doubling to 15 min; sessions are HttpOnly cookies (idle 30 min, max 12 h; "keep me signed in" 7 / 30 days); owners give their password again before account changes (counts 10 min). Passwords are PBKDF2 hashes, tokens SHA-256, authenticator secrets sealed with the app key (Secrets Manager). New people get an invite link (7 days) to choose a password and connect their phone; a lost phone or password is an owner's "Reset sign-in" (old ones stop at once, 24-hour link). API calls need `x-wms: 1` and JSON, so other sites can't act as someone.
- **Roles** (`server/src/shared/permissions.ts`): three built-in roles (switches change, names don't) plus roles a warehouse makes (a name, a base role, the switches). Owner-only, never switchable: change stock directly, decide returns, apply count corrections, delete counts, manage people and roles, warehouse settings, support access, security log. The app's `can()` and the server use the same file.
- **Data on the server** (`server/src/core/sync.ts`, `app/src/sync/`; design doc section 2): the warehouse's data is stored piece by piece with versions (`entities`). A device works a change out with the app's own logic (`root` in `store/store.tsx`, which takes its time from the action), sends the changed pieces with their versions (`sync.push`), and the server checks the role's switch for that kind of change and the kinds of piece it may touch (`ACTION_RULES` in `server/src/shared/sync.ts`; add every new store action there), checks that each action that can move stock (move, slot correction, tasks, picks, deliveries, counts, office order changes, returns; not the owners' own powers) changes only what that action does (`server/src/core/sync-checks.ts`; change it with the reducer), and refuses it if a piece changed meanwhile; the device then catches up (`sync.pull`, every 2 s in front) and works it out again. Direct and thread messages go only to members. Changes wait on the device while offline, encrypted with the person's `cacheKey`. Every device still gets all of the warehouse's stock (brand limits are on screen).
- **Setting a warehouse up** (Team → Setup, owners, password again; `server/src/core/setup.ts`): the settings, product list and logos our team prepares (A3K's are `app/public/tenants/a3k/`), products and opening stock from the `.xlsx` template, or the go-live count in Inventory → Count. Opening stock goes in through the sync with its arrival date (`StockLine.arrived`, used for FIFO). Owners can download a copy of everything they may see.
- **Onboarding** (Nov 2026): the public page's "Book a visit" is a three-step intake (contact; racks, pallet positions, staff, clients, products, how stock is tracked and how orders arrive; timing), kept in `visit_requests.details` (`server/src/core/site.ts`, migration `platform/0004_visit_intake.sql`); no files are uploaded there. In the console, Visits → "Create the warehouse" makes it from the request (linked by `tenant_id`) and invites its first owner with the request's name and email filled in. A new warehouse starts in **setup mode** (settings key `setup`; a warehouse without one is live): owners and our team see Manage warehouse → Get started (`pages/manage/SetupGuide.tsx`: steps ticked by the data where possible, by hand for the site survey, labels and practice), every home and Work screen says it's setup mode, "Clear practice data" removes stock, deliveries, orders, tasks, returns and counts (the stock history and change log keep a note; they're never edited), and "Go live" ends setup mode for good (optionally clearing practice orders, deliveries, tasks and returns; stock and counts stay). The layout editor (Manage warehouse → Racks, `pages/manage/LayoutEditor.tsx`) edits column types, racks, odd columns, junctions and floor areas with a live map; its checks (`layoutProblems` in `server/src/shared/layout.ts`, the layout code's home now, re-exported by `app/src/tenant/layout.ts`) run on the server too, which refuses any layout that would strand stock or an open task and, once live, a change to existing racks without a reason (security log). The opening-stock import refuses locations the layout doesn't have.
- **Backups** (`server/src/ops/backup.ts`): every night at 08:00 UTC each warehouse's database is dumped on its own into the files bucket (`tenants/<id>/backups/`), encrypted with its own key, kept 35 days; a failure emails the alert address. A warehouse's NAS may get a read-only user for its own backups (`nasTenants`).
- **Order emails** (`server/src/ops/inbound.ts`): `<id>@orders.<domain>` → SES → files bucket + queue → the API → the warehouse's inbox, once per email; PDFs up to 1 MB are kept and read by the app when drafting; senders not in `orders.trustedSenders` arrive ignored.
- **Deploy settings** come from GitHub environment variables (`WMS_DOMAIN`, `WMS_CERTIFICATE_ARN`, …; docs/aws-setup.md).
- **The client portal** (`<warehouse address>/portal`, `server/src/core/portal-api.ts`, migration `0006_client_portal.sql`): clients' logins are their own tables in the warehouse's database (`client_accounts`, `client_sessions`, `client_tickets`, `client_invites`; `CLIENT_TABLES` in `auth.ts`), one client each, same sign-in rules (password + authenticator code, lockouts, invite and reset links), and their own cookie (`wms-portal`, API `POST /portal/api/<method>`). So a client session opens no warehouse method and a warehouse session opens no portal method (`server/test/portal.test.ts`). A login sees only its client's active products (code, name, size, units; never stock or slots) and what its client's logins sent (`portal_submissions`), with the status only. Orders become `order:` drafts (source `portal`) and delivery notices `shipment:` deliveries in Incoming (one line per pallet), written with `writeSystem` plus a Logs line; a `requestId` saves a resend once. Owners make, reset and turn off logins in Manage warehouse → Clients (`clientLogins.*`, `manage-people`, password again); every step is in the security log.
- **The ops console** (`ops.<domain>`): the WMS team's accounts live in the platform database, never in a warehouse; a warehouse login doesn't work there and vice versa. It shows each warehouse's health and counts, never its stock, orders or messages. Looking inside needs a support grant: asked for in the console, approved by an owner on their Team page (time-limited, look-only unless allowed, ended by any owner), opened through a one-time link; emergency access opens at once and emails every owner. Restoring an owner's access and creating warehouses (starts the one-off task) need the operator's password again. Everything is in the platform log and the warehouse's security log.

## Owner decisions for the full WMS (Oct 2026)

- **What must be flawless** (the warehouse, 3 Nov 2026): in-bounds (reporting a delivery and seeing it update live on every screen) and keeping the inventory right. Everything else is a variation of these or branches from them, so changes there get the most care and testing. The test plan (`docs/test-plan.md`, report `docs/test-report-template.md`) puts them first as critical checks C1–C3, run three times with no fails allowed.
- **Product name: Lattice** (22 Oct 2026). Logo: `Lattice_Branding/` (the current one is `LatticeLogo_Blank(1).png`, a rounded square and "Lattice."; the app uses it as `app/src/assets/lattice-logo.png`, and the word alone as `lattice-word.png`, both as masks in the text colour, `components/LatticeLogo.tsx`; the rounded square alone is the icon, `app/public/lattice-icon.svg`). Branding stays light inside the WMS: the warehouse's own name and mark lead; Lattice shows on the landing page, the console, small under sign-in, and as the tab icon.

- **Platform**: installable web app (React + TypeScript), managed AWS services with PostgreSQL, real-time updates for several users at once, nightly backup to the NAS. Phones keep working offline and sync later, with a visible saved/syncing icon.
- **Sign-in**: email/username and password plus two-factor for everyone.
- **Devices**: warehouse-owned devices; workers may also use their own phones.
- **Every stock change** needs a reason and is recorded permanently. History is visible only to certain roles.
- **Inbound** records: brand, supplier, SKU, lot number, best-before date, cases, number of pallets, shipment (arrival) date, container or BOL number, who received it, condition. Quantities are in cases; each pallet holds a number of cases.
- **Confirmation**: a higher role (not a worker) confirms each received pallet. The slot is assigned right after confirmation and saved to the cloud at once.
- **Damage report**: photos, quantity and reason, exportable as a PDF for the supplier.
- **Put-away**: racks hold set product types (list to come). The system suggests a slot near similar products; a person may choose another slot, with the reason logged. When a type's racks are full, overflow goes to the floor of the rack where most of that product sits; certain roles can route it to a free slot instead. Mixed pallets (two or more products on one pallet) are allowed.
- **QR codes** (owner, 23 Oct 2026): one per rack and one per column, not per slot. They hold only an address (`<warehouse address>/rack/06`, `<warehouse address>/06B-02`). A rack's opens its columns as big numbers; a column's opens the column level by level; tapping a slot shows what's on it and lets people with the "Correct a slot after scanning it" switch (`scan-adjust`, on for every role by default) change the case counts with a reason, logged for the owners (`stock/scan-adjust`). Sign-in comes first. Labels are printed from Manage warehouse → Racks (`pages/manage/QrLabels.tsx`); the pages are `pages/slot/SlotPage.tsx` (`#/slot/rack/<id>`, `#/slot/<column>`, `#/slot/<slot>`).
- **Expiry**: warning time set per product category (e.g. rice 6 months, snacks 2 months). Alerts on the home page, by email, and by text for some, with per-user settings. Expired stock is shown in black, can't be picked, and raises an alert.
- **Quantities**: always in cases. "Units" means products per case and is stored as product information (for labels and orders). Loose items such as single rice bags are still recorded in cases.
- **Inventory reports**: on demand; the user chooses the scope (per brand, per store, etc.) and the sort (product code, lot, expiry, quantity, shipment date). Download as `.xlsx`; no Google account connection.
- **Count mode**: under Inventory. Scan a slot, enter what's there, see the difference; an owner approves corrections. Counts happen when an owner decides.
- **Orders**: from stores, by PDF upload or emailed to a dedicated inbox (e.g. `orders@a3kimports.com`); the app drafts the order for approval. Each order has a due date, from the slip or typed in. The order page highlights the pallets to pick on the map.
- **Partial pallets**: an order may take cases off a pallet. Keep partial pallets to a minimum: take extra cases from existing partial pallets first, and only open a full pallet when no partial pallet is left.
- **Returns**: their own pages under In-bounds. A return stays pending and doesn't change inventory until it's approved.
- **Go-live**: build and test the full WMS on localhost with sample inventories first. Then do one full count over 3 days and load it into the app; the spreadsheets stop that day (no parallel run).
- **Labels**: printed with the Order Label Maker (and the app's Labels tab).
- **Roles** (owner-defined, 14 Oct 2026). The admin assigns the role when approving an account request.
    - **Owners**: everything is visible and editable, but not easily (direct changes need a review step and a reason). A **Logs** section records every change: the action, who made it, and the date and time.
    - **Coordinators**: only they (and owners) confirm in-bound quantities; they oversee the workers' pallet log, in-bounds, orders and all inventory, see where every pallet is, print labels for orders, confirm orders before they leave, send/report counts and in-bound reports, and always have a live stock count of every brand.
    - **Workers**: only move pallets (in-bound dock → rack, rack → out-bound, rack → rack), always with a slot given by the WMS, and confirm each pallet once it's in the rack. They pack orders given to them by coordinators. They have their own dashboard (time, their pallet tasks and packing, message notifications) and work devices mounted on forklifts showing their pallet tasks, the time and notifications.
